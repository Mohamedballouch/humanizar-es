\begin{abstract}
Remote work has fundamentally reshaped how organizations think about productivity.
We study 1,200 employees across 14 firms and find that hybrid schedules raise
self-reported output by 7.5\% while reducing commuting time by 52 minutes per day.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The rapid expansion of remote work over the past several years has changed the way
organizations plan collaboration~\cite{smith2020,lee2021}. As shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:trend}, adoption grew from 12\% in 2019 to 41\% in 2022. % TODO: update
Prior work by Mart\'{\i}nez et al.~\cite{martinez2019} measured output with a simple
model $y = \alpha + \beta x$, where $\beta$ captures the effect of distance.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A panel of 1,200 employees observed over 24 months in \emph{14} firms.
  \item An estimate of the effect of hybrid schedules on output, see Eq.~\eqref{eq:main}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
We estimate the following model:
\begin{equation}
  y_{it} = \alpha_i + \beta \, \text{hybrid}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
  \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
where $y_{it}$ is the output of employee $i$ in month $t$. Standard errors are
clustered by firm, and the sample excludes the first 3 months of 2020.

\section{Conclusion}
Ultimately, there is no one-size-fits-all answer. Hybrid schedules raised output by
7.5\% in our sample, a result that holds across all 14 firms ($p < 0.01$).
